# The Stony Path

> The farmer wants his crops to grow.
> He enters the field to sow, sow, sow.
> But the seed falls onto the path below.
> On dry dirt there's no room to grow.
> So the roots are short and too shallow.

Use the uploaded illustration as the starting frame. Preserve the original hand-carved black-and-white linocut exactly — same bold relief-print shapes, carved gouge marks, stark high contrast, proportions, and composition. Pure black ink on white paper, no colour and no grey shading. Do not redraw or reinterpret the print. Camera completely static — no pan, zoom, tilt, or cuts.

Begin with a few carved corn seeds scattering down from off the top of the frame — flung by the unseen sower — arcing through the air and striking the packed white stones, bouncing and skittering across the hard surface before coming to rest on top of the rock, nowhere to sink in. The seeds are cut in the same bold linocut style, pure black on white. Then the action begins.

The carved sun in the top-left glares down over the stony ground. Its face shifts almost imperceptibly and its ring of spiky rays flickers outward in slow pulses. The hard horizontal bands of the sky drift very slightly, like heat shimmer pressed into the block.

In the centre the little seedling sits helpless on top of the packed white stones. Its pale shoot and two small leaves quiver and strain upward toward the sun, then wilt a fraction, drooping. Its thin roots splay sideways across the surface of the stones, twitching and searching for a crack to enter but finding none — never digging in. One or two pebbles settle with a tiny shift.

The dense field of carved stones stays otherwise still, a dry mosaic filling the frame.

End on the same parched composition, the seedling's leaves drooping under the staring sun.

**Sound:** a light scatter of seed striking and skittering across bare stone at the open, then a dry hot wind over stone, the faint tick and settle of pebbles, a thin brittle rustle from the failing shoot. No dialogue, narration, singing, or music.

**Style:** faithful to the original linocut / woodblock relief print, bold black-and-white folk-art woodcut aesthetic, subtle print-like movement only. No new objects, no colour, no added shading, no camera moves. Movement stays slow, dry, and struggling.
