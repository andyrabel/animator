# A Hundredfold

> The farmer wants his crops to grow.
> He enters the field to sow, sow, sow.
> And some seed falls on ground fallow,
> And digs its roots in deep below,
> And bears its fruit a hundredfold.

Preserve the original hand-carved black-and-white linocut exactly — same bold relief-print shapes, carved gouge marks, stark high contrast, proportions. Pure black ink on white paper, no colour and no grey shading. Do not redraw or reinterpret the print. The only camera move is a single slow, steady zoom out — no pan, tilt, or cuts.

Begin tight on the bare carved furrow-lines of the fallow ground. A few carved corn seeds scatter down from off the top of the frame — flung by the unseen sower — arcing through the air and dropping into the soft furrow, settling into the earth. The seeds are cut in the same bold linocut style, pure black on white. Then hold on the two heavy ears of ripe corn standing full and swollen in the foreground, matching the uploaded illustration exactly. They sway gently on the stalk in a light breeze, turning a fraction toward the viewer as if offered up. The peeled-back husks and their long leaf-blades lift and curl slowly, rustling; the rows of carved kernels seem to swell and settle, plump with grain, and a fine dusting of carved flecks drifts down like falling chaff.

Across the shot the camera pulls slowly and smoothly back. The two cobs shrink into the frame and the view widens to reveal that they stand at the front of a whole field — rank on rank of the same carved corn stalks marching back in furrowed rows, every stalk heavy with cobs, a hundredfold harvest standing thick to the horizon. Keep every added stalk in the identical linocut style, carved in the same bold gouged lines, pure black on white.

The long carved grain-lines of the field ripple slowly as the view opens out.

End on the wide view: the two first cobs small in the foreground and the vast carved field of ripe corn stretching away behind them.

**Sound:** a light patter of scattered seed dropping into soft earth at the open, then a soft warm breeze, the papery rustle of corn husks, the dry whisper of a ripe field, the faint patter of falling chaff. No dialogue, narration, singing, or music.

**Style:** faithful to the original linocut / woodblock relief print, bold black-and-white folk-art woodcut aesthetic, subtle print-like movement only. Any field revealed by the zoom out must be carved in the same woodcut style — no colour, no added shading, no photographic detail. The one slow zoom out is the only camera move. Movement stays slow, generous, and full.
