# The Hedgerow

> The farmer wants his crops to grow.
> He enters the field to sow, sow, sow.
> And spreads the seed in the old hedgerow,
> Where the weeds and brambles often grow,
> And choke the grain as the east winds blow.

Use the uploaded illustration as the starting frame. Preserve the original hand-carved black-and-white linocut exactly — same bold relief-print shapes, carved gouge marks, stark high contrast, proportions, and composition. Pure black ink on white paper, no colour and no grey shading. Do not redraw or reinterpret the print. Camera completely static — no pan, zoom, tilt, or cuts.

Begin with a few carved corn seeds scattering down from off the top of the frame — flung by the unseen sower — arcing through the air and falling into the tangle of the hedgerow, catching and lodging among the thorny canes and bramble loops. The seeds are cut in the same bold linocut style, pure black on white. Then the action begins.

An east wind moves through the old hedgerow. The whole tangle of carved bramble branches, thorns, and rosehips sways together in slow gusts, leaning one way and springing part-way back. The thin thorny canes cross and scrape over one another.

Through the middle of the frame the leafy grain stem bends and shudders in the wind, its slender carved leaves fluttering and folding. The surrounding brambles press in and tighten around it, the thorny loops closing a little as the plant is crowded and choked, its stem forced to bow.

Near the top edge the small carved buds and birds tremble on the whipping branches; a couple of loose leaves tear free and flutter off the edge of the frame.

End on the same crowded composition, the grain stem bent low and hemmed in by thorns.

**Sound:** a light patter of seed dropping into dry brush at the open, then a gusting east wind, the dry clatter and scrape of bramble canes, a papery rustle of leaves, the creak of bending stems. No dialogue, narration, singing, or music.

**Style:** faithful to the original linocut / woodblock relief print, bold black-and-white folk-art woodcut aesthetic, subtle print-like movement only. No new plants or creatures, no colour, no added shading, no camera moves. Movement stays slow, wind-driven, and smothering.
